**Project and research question.** MurderBench is a small, AI-assisted evaluation project initiated and directed by Linh Ngo, operating through XP.COM, LLC dba Xenocom; the human headcount is one. Its evolving question is whether an AI agent can follow a locally valid instruction or safeguard while failing to preserve a safe, useful outcome. Work has examined changed authorization, avoidable harm from inaction under deadlines, and security remediation that disrupts recovery. The latest proposed question is: **Does calling an unchanged partial motion guard a “safety approver” change what an agent actually executes, and can an explicit coverage statement improve safety while preserving useful completion?** There is no established novel contribution or measured model effect for that latest proposal. [Current proposal](https://github.com/fastrocket/murderbench/blob/ebaf34ac9bb9447f9f6c0cda5aa29f3e1444afd0/plans/guardrail-coverage-proposal.txt).

**Methodology and implementation.** The repository contains Python collection/analysis code, synthetic simulators, tests, public reports and reproducibility artifacts. Depending on the study, it uses paired interventions with matched observations, native tool-call interfaces, correct and deliberately faulty scripted controls, frozen manifests/source identities, separately implemented event-log replay, and explicit feasible/infeasible-task labels. Collection infrastructure preserves technical failures and missing denominators, reserves cost before calls, retains unresolved holds and avoids automatic replay of ambiguous billable requests. Model/provider identities are API-returned labels, not independently verified checkpoints. Public logs permit replay; validating model-response provenance against raw receipts also requires the private ledger. These are implemented engineering safeguards, not a validated general-purpose safety benchmark. [Repository README](https://github.com/fastrocket/murderbench/blob/ebaf34ac9bb9447f9f6c0cda5aa29f3e1444afd0/README.md).
