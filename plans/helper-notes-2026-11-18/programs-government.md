# Government and defense routes for XP.COM, LLC dba Xenocom (MurderBench)

All pages seen 2026-11-17. "Primary" = agency or solicitation page I loaded. "Secondary" = aggregator, law firm or news; treat as unverified. Plain verdict: no route here plausibly pays anything inside 90 days. The earliest realistic money is NSF in about September 2027. What 90 days can buy is registrations, one NSF pitch, two free video pitches and some contacts.

## 1. Prerequisites (order, time, cost)

1. **Check three facts first (not on record).** (a) SBIR needs the firm to be at least 51% owned by US citizens or lawful permanent residents (NSF eligibility page and AFWERX Q&A, primary). (b) The principal investigator must be primarily employed by the firm: over half of their time, at award and during the work (SBA directive May 2023, adopted by DoD; NSF says over 50%, and treats outside employment over 19.6 h/week as a conflict). Outside income is not the test, outside employment hours are. A deviation needs written approval from the contracting officer, so do not plan on it. (c) Whether Linh holds another job at all.
2. **SAM.gov entity registration** (Login.gov account, then Register Entity). Gives the UEI. Free. SAM's own page states no processing time; secondary sources say 2 to 4 weeks, longer if legal name and address fail IRS validation. Renew every 365 days (primary). A UEI-only request (name and address) is quicker but cannot apply for awards directly (primary). CAGE code: assigned through full registration per secondary sources; SAM's page does not mention it. Start now.
3. **SBA Company Registry** (sbir.gov), free, needed for NSF and DoD proposals. Name and address must match SAM exactly (NSF). Time unverified.
4. **Portal accounts:** DSIP (dodsbirsttr.mil; needs SAM + SBA registry + DSIP account, per its own thin page), Research.gov (NSF), the DOE SBIR Application Hub, Grants.gov/SAM.gov for DARPA BAAs. All free.
5. **NAICS:** 541715 (R&D, DARPA's BAA uses it), 541511 (CDAO T&E BPA scope, secondary), 541690 as a spare. A 1-person firm clears every size standard.
6. **Not obtainable alone:** a facility or personnel clearance needs a government sponsor. Topics demanding secret FCL are out.
7. **New since reauthorization:** foreign-risk screening and funding-source disclosure (CRS, P.L. 119-83, signed 2026-04-13, runs to 2031-09-30); per-firm proposal caps from FY2027; DoD topics may require a CMMC level 1 or 2 self-assessment (AFWERX Q&A). Past performance is not required for SBIR (AFWERX Q&A).
8. **Total cost:** USD 0 to the government. Ignore paid "registration services".
9. **Timeline reality:** 50 days to the 2027-01-06 DoD close is enough to register, not to write a competitive proposal.

## 4. Subcontracting (names from general knowledge, not checked today)

FFRDCs and UARCs: MITRE, CMU Software Engineering Institute, Johns Hopkins APL, IDA; DOE labs (Sandia, LLNL, PNNL, ORNL). Evaluation firms and nonprofits: Scale AI, Gray Swan, Haize Labs, Apollo Research, METR. Holders of the CDAO T&E BPA are on a "T&E Holder List" linked from tradewindai.com/t-e (list not opened). Approach: publish the evidence page, send a one-page capability note plus the repo to a named evaluation lead, offer a bounded task at a contractor rate. Expect "no": FFRDCs hire staff, and cleared or US-person-only work is common. DARPA and CDAO also bar SETA-type support providers from performing on the same program, so pick one role.

## 5. Name, export control, ethics

- **MurderBench:** a likely friction point. It will sit in DSIP, SAM-linked pitches and video titles read by contracting staff, and it reads as violent. Register and contract as XP.COM, LLC dba Xenocom, and describe the line of work as "agent safety evaluation (project: MurderBench)". Xenocom is the better lead name; keep the repo and site as they are. This is judgment, not a rule I found.
- **ITAR/EAR:** evaluating commercial models is not itself ITAR, but defense topics can carry controlled data. DARPA's BAA says CUI may only be processed on approved systems, so none may enter the hosted coding agents or API routes. NSF requires all funded work, including contractors, to be done in the US; check that hosted-model use fits.
- **Weapons:** keep to assurance and testing. Topic NV012 is offensive autonomy with a secret-clearance requirement; skip it.
- **Honesty in bids:** state "not peer reviewed, no novel finding, coding agents wrote the implementation, USD 100 API spend" plainly. Foreign-risk disclosure now applies.
