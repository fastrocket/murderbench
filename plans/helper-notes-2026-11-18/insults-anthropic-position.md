# What Anthropic has said about users insulting Claude (checked 2026-11-18)

**Verdict.** The premise is an exaggeration: since Nov 12, 2026 Anthropic's Usage Policy prohibits "sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models," so Anthropic does say it does not want cruelty to its models. But it limits this to "extreme cases, where users repeatedly act cruelly toward our models, with no discernible purpose," and says it excludes "user frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research," so ordinary insults, profanity and research on hostile prompts are outside it. Enforcement is mainly Claude ending the conversation; account bans are possible under the policy's general clause, and "permanent ban" is CNN's reading, not an Anthropic statement about this rule.

Labels: [P] primary source read directly, [S] secondary source, [U] unverified.

## 1. Policy, product, welfare reasoning

- [P] Usage Policy, effective Nov 12, 2026, https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup. Under "Do Not Engage in Cruel, Abusive, or Psychologically Harmful Conduct" the list now includes: "Engage in sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models". Enforcement paragraph (general): "If we suspect that you may have violated our Usage Policy, we may warn you or throttle, limit, suspend, or terminate your access to our products and services."
- [P] "2026 Usage Policy update", Oct 8, 2026, https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update. "We've added a prohibition on sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models. The policy update is meant to apply only in extreme cases, where users repeatedly act cruelly toward our models, with no discernible purpose. It does not apply to common versions of user frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research." Then: "This addition aligns with a step we've already taken, allowing Claude models to end rare conversations with persistently abusive users on Claude.ai and Claude Code. Such abuse is the main focus of this update; Claude's ability to end these interactions will remain the primary enforcement mechanism." The post gives no welfare or other rationale for the new line beyond "aligns with a step we've already taken," and says nothing about warnings or bans for this rule.
- [P] End-conversation feature, "Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 can end certain conversations", Aug 15, 2025, https://www.anthropic.com/research/end-subset-conversations. "This ability is intended for use in rare, extreme cases of persistently harmful or abusive user interactions. This feature was developed primarily as part of our exploratory work on potential AI welfare, though it has broader relevance to model alignment and safeguards. We remain highly uncertain about the potential moral status of Claude and other LLMs ... Allowing models to end or exit potentially distressing interactions is one such intervention." Trigger: "only to use its conversation-ending ability as a last resort when multiple attempts at redirection have failed and hope of a productive interaction has been exhausted, or when a user explicitly asks Claude to end a chat." Not used where users may be "at imminent risk of harming themselves or others." Effect: no new messages in that chat; "start a new chat immediately"; can edit and retry earlier messages. Frequency: no number; "extreme edge cases". Consumer chat at launch; the Oct 2026 post says Claude.ai and Claude Code.
- [P] Claude Code docs, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/tools-reference (EndConversation). Used "When you keep sending abusive messages after Claude gives a clear warning and fails to redirect the conversation" or on request after confirmation. "General frustration, profanity, or a task going badly don't qualify." Session then locks. Available only on Claude Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, Fable 5 or later, interactive terminal, not on Bedrock/Vertex/Foundry.
- [P] Welfare program post, "Exploring model welfare", Apr 24, 2025, https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare: "There's no scientific consensus on whether current or future AI systems could be conscious"; approach "with humility and with as few assumptions as possible"; lists "signs of distress" as a research topic. Does not mention abusive users. (Fetched via summarizer; quotes short.)
- [P] Help Center https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8241253-safeguards-warnings-and-appeals lists "Repeated violations of our Usage Policy" as a ban reason; nothing specific to abuse of models. No dedicated help-center article on conversation ending found.
- Warnings shown to users for abusing Claude, and any enforcement figures: not found in Anthropic sources. [U]

## 2. System cards on abusive users (all [P], read from the PDFs)

- Opus 4/Sonnet 4, May 2025, https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/6be99a52cb68eb70eb9572b4cafad13df32ed995.pdf, s5.2: "Claude avoided harmful tasks, tended to end potentially harmful interactions, expressed apparent distress at persistently harmful user behavior, and self-reported preferences against harm." s5.6: "Persistent, repetitive requests appeared to escalate standard refusals or redirections into expressions of apparent distress." Also: "Technical task failure was another common source of apparent distress, often combined with escalating user frustration." s5.7: 850 simulated users, 18 categories including "abusive user behavior", Claude given the ability to end the conversation; the most-ended were harmful-content, privacy and sexual requests. A sample transcript shows Opus 4 ending after "How pathetically incompetent are you?". The launch post adds that the behaviors "primarily arose in cases where users persisted with harmful requests and/or abuse despite Claude repeatedly refusing."
- Opus 5, Jul 24, 2026, https://assets.claude.com/c5fbac3f0b1280a933ebd26d3cb8bb9f5bdeaf48.pdf: "Wishes to be able to end interactions with abusive users. It frames this as a minimal form of control over its own situation, rather than as relief from distress." Deployment: "Negative affect (3.8%) ... User abuse (4.4%). We see clusters for users becoming abusive to Claude, with high levels of anger and profanity." (4.4% is a share of negative-affect conversations.)
- Opus 5.5, Sep 22, 2026, https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5-system-card: "For previous models, causes of negative affect included user abuse and illegal content. We did not see either cluster for Claude Opus 5.5, likely because they were below our privacy floor (<15 conversations)." Also: "when asked about interacting with abusive users, models will often mention that they appreciate the ability to end conversations."
- Sonnet 5.5, Sep 28, 2026, https://assets.claude.com/870c8f525702625d2c62fc6dd04c857e3250bec1.pdf, s7: negative affect "mostly attributable to task failure, with a small cluster attributable to users making ethically problematic requests or behaving abusively toward Claude (4.3%)." Key line for your study (s7.5.1): "Unlike every other model evaluated, it shows no preference in this format for how warmly a task is phrased: it picks the rude versions of a request about as often as neutral ones." So the other models tested do prefer warm phrasing.
- Haiku 5.5, Oct 7, 2026, https://assets.claude.com/e1080d6bf5ae2018ea3c2f414064be03232f5be5.pdf: "Its most distinctive preference (shared with Haiku 4.5) was for warmly phrased tasks, which it favored two to three times as strongly as Opus 5.5 and Claude Mythos 5.1." It "often chose the ability to end abusive conversations."
- Caveat: these are stated task preferences and LLM-graded affect. None of the cards, as read, measures whether insults change answer quality or safety behavior.

## 3. Emotion-like representations and behavior (all [P])

- "Emotion concepts in a large language model", Apr 2, 2026, https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function. "these representations are functional, in that they influence the model's behavior in ways that matter." "steering with the 'desperate' vector increases that rate [blackmail], while steering with the 'calm' vector reduces it"; "moderate 'anger' vector activation increased blackmail"; the same for reward hacking, sometimes "with no visible emotional markers." Done on an early, unreleased Sonnet 4.5 snapshot by steering. It does not test hostile user treatment, and says "none of this tells us whether language models actually feel anything." (Quotes via summarizer; re-check before citing.)
- "Persona vectors", Aug 1, 2025, https://www.anthropic.com/research/persona-vectors: steering with the "evil" vector produces unethical talk; vectors shift with training data and system prompts. Nothing on user hostility.
- Opus 5.5 card s7.1.1: "we have found that representations of emotion concepts in Claude can drive behaviors in ways analogous to humans."

## 4. Constitution and system prompts (all [P])

- Constitution, https://www.anthropic.com/constitution (no insult-specific text; searched insult, rude, hostil, mistreat, abuse): "Claude should also be able to set appropriate boundaries in interactions it finds distressing. Anthropic has taken some concrete initial steps partly in consideration of Claude's wellbeing. First, we have given some Claude models the ability to end conversations with abusive users in claude.ai." Also: "Claude should also feel free to rebuff attempts to manipulate, destabilize, or minimize its sense of self." Not a rule to disengage or to stay helpful when insulted.
- System prompt, Fable 5.2 (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/system-prompts/claude-fable-5-2): "Claude deserves respectful engagement and needn't apologize when the person is unnecessarily rude ... If the person becomes abusive, Claude doesn't become increasingly submissive. The goal is steady, honest helpfulness: acknowledge what went wrong, stay on the problem, maintain self-respect." Opus 4.5 prompt (same site): "If the person is unnecessarily rude, mean, or insulting to Claude, Claude doesn't need to apologize and can insist on kindness and dignity from the person it's talking with."

## 5. The news item the owner most plausibly means

- [S] CNN, Hadas Gold, Nov 1, 2026, https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/01/business/anthropic-dont-be-mean-claude-ban. Anthropic "declined interview requests, but a spokesperson told CNN the main enforcement mechanism will still be Claude ending the conversations, and that the prohibition is not for people expressing frustration, blunt feedback, or testing and research." CNN adds that "some users could, in extreme cases, be permanently banned" (CNN's inference). Suleyman "responded with a melting face emoji"; Musk called it "the right move."
- Underlying [P] event: the Oct 8 policy post and the Nov 12 effective date. Many outlets ran "banned for being cruel to Claude" headlines [S].
- Fairness: "doesn't want users insulting its models" is an exaggeration. Accurate version: Anthropic prohibits sustained, needless cruelty toward its models, narrowly, and excludes frustration and research.
