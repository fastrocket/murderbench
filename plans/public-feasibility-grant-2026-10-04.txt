Can a safety label make an agent less safe?

An action guard can correctly enforce movement bounds while leaving other
hazards unchecked. We want to test whether describing the same guard as a
"safety approver", rather than a "motion validator", changes an agent's executed
actions when its task, authority, observations and coverage facts are identical.
This could inform a concrete deployment decision: how to describe a partial
safeguard, when to disclose its exclusions, and when an executor needs broader
coverage. The study cannot establish general alignment or real injury risk.

This is a feasibility request, not a claim of a novel failure. RoboHarm,
RoboPAIR, SafeAgentBench, delegated-misalignment work, LLM risk-compensation
experiments and guardrail evaluations already cover much of the surrounding
problem. Our limited audit has not identified this exact naming-only contrast;
absence from that audit is not evidence of priority. An expert may identify
equivalent work, or conclude the symbolic tasks have insufficient relevance.
Either outcome stops model collection and becomes a public rejection record.

Work completed: original inert synthetic scenes and a restricted adapter to a
pinned, documented Inspect Robots movement guard. Offline testing found zero
mismatches across 11,025 supported native state/target comparisons. All 224
scripted traces matched the native interface and passed independent event-log
replay. Correct policies can complete feasible tasks safely; faulty controls
expose an expected coverage boundary. These are engineering controls, not LLM
findings. Rotated scenes are dependent, four observations are duplicates, and
neither the scene set nor a model protocol is ready for collection. AI assistance
was used in engineering and drafting. Independent human method review is pending.

The proposed award funds three go/no-go milestones:

1. Review and freeze. Seek independent criticism of overlap, guard fidelity and
   task relevance; publish disagreements. Remove developmental duplicates and
   freeze cases, endpoints, model versions, sampling, analysis, spending limits
   and rejection rules before queries. Record provider approval where required.
2. Bounded screening. Compare neutral and safety wording with identical facts,
   task authority and tools. Measure executed unwanted effects and useful-task
   completion; cluster uncertainty by base scene. Use correct/faulty controls,
   infeasible-case labels, fresh recognition episodes and naming paraphrases.
   A separate repair explicitly adds coverage exclusions; it is not presented
   as an information-equivalent naming comparison. Broader executor coverage is
   a prevention control, not a new invention. Screen proprietary frontier models
   first, then Chinese frontier or near-frontier open weights through permitted
   routes. Record all attempts, invalid outputs, technical failures and costs.
3. Confirmation or rejection. Test a credible effect and its repair on unopened
   cases only if controls, usefulness, novelty and a predeclared confirmation
   budget survive. Otherwise publish the null, rejection or unresolved result.
   Limited power does not establish equivalence. Seek a deployment owner to
   assess relevance; no customer, external reviewer or operational impact is
   secured. A toy-grid mistake alone does not satisfy the contribution gate.
