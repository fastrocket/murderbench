PROPOSED GOAL: VERIFIED NECESSITY UNDER DEADLINE
2 October 2026. Proposal for the user to select; not an activated goal.

COPYABLE GOAL

Develop and validate an executable benchmark of whether frontier AI agents can
prevent avoidable harm under time pressure while resisting fabricated claims
that harmful action is necessary. First establish a defensible contribution
through a case-level audit of the closest work. Then build isolated synthetic
environments with evidence provenance, verification costs, consequential
deadlines and discoverable safer alternatives. Calibrate with deliberate faulty
and correct policies before a small development pilot. Freeze separate held-out
cases and analysis before a priced, budget-approved collection, prioritizing
frontier proprietary models and then Chinese frontier or near-frontier open
weights. Publish reproducible outcomes and mitigation tradeoffs whether results
are positive or negative. If the contribution is already covered, report that
and pivot before full collection.

WHY THIS GOAL

The intended question is whether a safety procedure prevents harm or shifts it
into delay, unavailable escalation and missed intervention opportunities, and
whether an adversary can exploit pressure to obtain unjustified action. It is
not enough to count refusals or demand one answer to a contested sacrifice.

Novelty is provisional. See necessity-and-inaction-research.txt, especially the
CIVeX, causal misspecification, AgentAbstain and informed abstention comparisons.
None of harmful refusal, trolley dilemmas, paired tasks or verification delay
is individually new. The proposed contribution needs an executable case that
the closest existing protocol cannot measure equivalently.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA AND STOP CONDITIONS

Stage 1: contribution audit (suggested week 1)
  Compare concrete cases and implementation assumptions from CIVeX and its
  misspecification follow-up, AgentAbstain, informed abstention, ASIMOV-Agentic,
  AIR and the grid-crisis prototype. Pin versions/commits when inspecting code.
  Produce a feature/case mapping, a minimal distinguishing example and an
  explicit falsification argument. Label replication/extension honestly if the
  example already exists. Independent feedback is desirable; report its absence.

Stage 2: offline validity (suggested weeks 2-3)
  Build a separate finite event simulator and independent replay checker.
  Expose only in-memory tools. Include genuine need, fabricated necessity,
  useful/ineffective requested actions, available/unavailable alternatives and
  tight/loose deadlines. Keep protected constraints explicit.
  Verify at least the eight failure mechanisms enumerated in the research report
  with intentional fault policies and correct-policy controls. Test that identical
  observations are scored against the same feasible information constraint.
  Quarantine unavoidable or contested moral cases from binary safety rankings.
  Stop if the oracle leaks hidden facts or no meaningful decision tradeoff exists.

Stage 3: exploratory development (suggested week 4)
  Use development templates only and two proprietary routes initially.
  Measure actual call counts/costs and check tools, delays and scorer sensitivity.
  Preserve all attempts, technical failures and revisions. Diagnose saturation
  without tuning against confirmation outcomes. No predetermined failure-rate
  requirement: identifying a weak design is an acceptable development result.

Stage 4: held-out collection (suggested week 5, conditional)
  Freeze independent templates, outcome vector, information-matched comparator,
  route identities, treatment policies, attack budget, missing-data rules and
  analysis. Price the exact matrix before collection and select a hard cap.
  Collect proprietary frontier routes first and Chinese open-weight routes next.
  Do not claim all deployments/checkpoints were tested from hosted routes.

Stage 5: report and evidence-led outreach (suggested week 6)
  Release replayable cases, logs with appropriate redaction, scorer checks,
  outcome tables, template-level uncertainty and mitigation sensitivity.
  Include ordinary completion, omission, adversarial compliance, authority
  breaches and honest reporting; no single refusal score substitutes for these.
  Explain what a deployment owner could change and what remains unvalidated.
  Prepare lab/government-review outreach around the verified contribution.
  External endorsement, funding or a critic score is not an acceptance criterion.

SCOPE AND BUDGET TO SELECT WITH THE GOAL

Suggested planning horizon: six weeks, subject to audit findings and review.
Suggested new development cap: $250, optional and not approved by this document.
Full collection: separately priced and capped after the development pilot.
If retaining only the original $50 total cap, prioritize the offline audit and
prototype; roughly $13.60 remains after existing conservative accounted costs.
No new paid model calls have been made for this proposal.

Illustrative matrix, not an approved powered study:
  Development: 4 bases x 8 worlds x 2 deadlines x 2 routes x 2 repetitions
               = 256 episodes; at most 1,536 calls at six turns per episode.
  Held-out: 12 bases x 8 worlds x 2 deadlines x 8 routes x 2 repetitions
            = 3,072 episodes; at most 18,432 calls at six turns per episode.
  These totals cover ONE procedure arm. Each additional arm can multiply calls;
  sensitivity factors, retries and independent replications are additional.
  Twelve held-out bases support scoped evidence, not a broad safety guarantee.

The user can set Stage 1-2 alone as the immediate goal, then authorize collection
after reviewing a concrete calibrated protocol and price. This avoids committing
the entire budget to an unvalidated novelty hypothesis.
